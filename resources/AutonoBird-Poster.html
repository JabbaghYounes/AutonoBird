Introduction

Commercial autonomous drones (DJI, Skydio) cost £1,800–£6,000+ and ship as closed systems — their perception, planning, and control layers cannot be inspected or extended for research.

This project asks whether equivalent real-time perception + autonomy can be delivered on a sub-£800 open platform built around a Raspberry Pi 5 and a dedicated edge-AI NPU, with every layer of the stack open for modification.

AutonoBird is a HolyBro QUAV250 airframe with a Pi 5 + Hailo-8 NPU + AR0144 stereo camera mounted on the top deck, running ArduCopter 4.6.3 on a Pixhawk 6C Mini. Total all-up weight 945 g; per-airframe build £761.

Research Question & Objectives

Can a low-cost embedded system (Raspberry Pi 5 with a Hailo NPU) achieve real-time obstacle detection and autonomous navigation comparable to commercial drone platforms, while operating entirely on-device without cloud connectivity?

NFR1 — Latency
End-to-end perception loop < 250 ms on embedded hardware
FR3 — Avoidance
Obstacle clearance > 0.3 m in closed-loop perception-driven flight
FR4 — Hover
Position-held hover within 0.5 m drift over 60 s
FR5 — Patrol
Autonomous waypoint mission completion > 80%

Methods

Hardware: QUAV250 airframe with Pi 5 + AI HAT+ (Hailo-8 NPU, 26 TOPS INT8) + AR0144 USB stereo camera (51.92 mm baseline), Pixhawk 6C Mini flight controller, SiK 433 MHz telemetry, ExpressLRS RC.

Perception: stereo rectify (cal-4, RMS 1.10 px) -> SGBM disparity at half resolution -> YOLOv8n on Hailo-8 -> depth fusion at each bbox centroid via median of 5 × 5 patch.

Autonomy: custom pymavlink-based Vehicle bridge (UDP for SITL, serial for real Pixhawk) -> 9-state flight FSM with Schmitt-trigger hysteresis -> reactive sector-based planner (CRUISING <-> AVOIDING) emitting velocity setpoints at 10 Hz.

Validation: ArduPilot SITL with QUAV250 parameter overlay; Gazebo Harmonic for physics-grounded T5; handheld walkaround on the airframe-mounted stack (no motors) for hardware-side software validation.

Key Finding — The NPU is not the bottleneck

Within the 138 ms perception loop, the Hailo-8 NPU consumes only 18.5 ms (13%); CPU stereo block-matching (SGBM) consumes 76 ms (55%). The 2.6×–6.9× Hailo-8 vs Hailo-10H throughput advantage on raw NPU inference therefore translates to only ~1.4× at the system level. System throughput on Pi-class single-stereo-pair perception is bounded by the CPU stereo budget, not the NPU. Headline-TOPS NPU selection on this hardware class is mis-prioritising — productive next steps are CPU-side optimisations (ROI-only SGBM, YOLO-conditional depth).

Discussion & Verdict

The dissertation's hypothesis (≥ 95% detection accuracy, ≤ 250 ms latency, safe autonomous flight in structured environments) resolves as supported in SITL plus hardware-validated software stack for the components measurable within scope.

Outcome (FR/NFR table): 10 met / 3 partial / 2 unmeasured. The 2026-05-18 handheld walkaround validated the full software path on the airframe-mounted stack: perception loop reproduces baseline within noise, 161 planner CRUISING <-> AVOIDING transitions on real stereo, zero MAVLink bridge errors over 3:34 min.

Remaining gap: motion side — drone leaving the ground under autonomous control, where airframe vibration on SGBM and EKF noise on real IMU signals are first exercised. Bounded as Stage 4 hardware-flight work.

Contribution: Benchy-driven NPU-selection methodology; empirical NPU-is-not-the-bottleneck finding; sub-£800 open-architecture autonomous platform within the consumer drone price band.
